# Review 1 of SPEC_DEPTH.md v1 (GPT-6.2 Astra, 2026-11-26)

**Revise before running.** The operator in §4 is the relative-site-units, identity-link construction used by DepthEmbedding; I verified that the two copies of `spacetime.py` are byte-identical. The main problems are downstream. A small check on real tokens failed the proposed replay control, two of the four questions lack the comparison they need, and the specified site payload exceeds its limit before most placements are counted.

References below to “the spec” are to `/data/code/TransformerModes/SPEC_DEPTH.md`.

### Findings

1. **Material — §5 and §7.1, lines 72–74, 101: the pilot did not test the placement path, and that path fails exact replay on real tokens.** `/data/tm_acts/pilot/depthpilot.py:70–72` passes the already fitted matrices `Ps` directly to `joint_forward`. Its roughly \(3\text{–}4\times10^{-14}\) error verifies the joint eigen-equation, not `dm.rows`, the self mask, cosine preprocessing, or token indexing. In `/data/code/DepthEmbedding/invert/dm.py:178–201`, construction picks exactly 15 forward neighbours before taking the union; replay admits **every** point at the reverse 15th-distance tie. Masking self does not resolve this discrepancy. On the first 512 text positions of Pythia `c00`, layer 0, I used the specified centring, the same cosine matrix for both paths, and masked its diagonal for replay: **170 of 512 rows differed by more than \(10^{-8}\) in total variation; the worst difference was 0.839.** Sinkhorn error was below \(10^{-15}\). This matters especially for repeated IDs and the ID-only control; arbitrary tie choices can look like positional structure.  
   **Change:** separate the algebraic `joint_forward(Ps)` test from end-to-end masked replay from activations. Either use one explicitly specified tie convention in both construction and extension, declaring the departure from unchanged code, or retain upstream exactly and report tied-row replay discrepancies separately from a strict tie-free test. Include a repeated-ID fixture and a joint reordering of rows and columns to detect dependence on storage order. Do not meet the gate by substituting stored coordinates.

2. **Material — §1 Q3, §6–7, lines 94–109: the controls do not establish agreement beyond token identity for the joint modes.** The headline statistic compares **joint** coordinates; the Gaussian-ID control compares **independent single-layer** modes. A joint mode’s layer-0 slice already depends on all 17 layers. Its pilot overlap of 0.710 is not the overlap of the two embedding tables alone. Nor is one pair of Gaussian dictionaries a ceiling for agreement between arbitrary functions of token ID. An unrestricted permutation destroys ID as well as context.  
   **Change:** compute the *same* joint statistic after permuting one model’s **occurrences within current token ID**, keeping each occurrence’s entire layer trajectory together. Give a seeded reference distribution and the movable fraction; singletons cannot test a context effect. This asks whether matching the actual occurrences adds agreement beyond their ID, not whether the extra agreement is semantic. Keep unrestricted permutations and Gaussian dictionaries as separately labelled references. If a joint synthetic baseline is wanted, identical walks admit an exact site-eigenvector × path-harmonic solution; `/data/code/TransformerModes/exp1/stage_b.py:419–440` already implements this case. Specify whether controls also cover placed texts and use the same rank, centring and rows as the real statistic.

3. **Material — §1 Q2, §§3, 5–6: changing the construction set has no direct comparison.** Different samples change document composition, token density per document, origin, graph, and calibrated \(t^*\). “Mode 3” in two settings need not denote the same direction. Likewise, the two cross-*model* scores for texts in `d32` and in `rand` do not tell us whether the *same model* represents a sequence similarly in those two constructions.  
   **Change:** freeze a common evaluation panel of named positions, outside all compared construction sets if possible. Compare adjacent ladder settings **within model** on those same rows, and `d32` versus `rand` on the same sequence, using rotation-invariant subspace overlap; show a scale-sensitive discrepancy too if claiming the clouds themselves agree. Label the relative coupling and actual \(t\). Treat the ladder as a description of these named samples, not an isolated effect of document count. The set’s own mean is consistent with the image method, but in a single document it subtracts that whole document’s mean, including future tokens. State that this is a retrospective geometry, not a correction to the activations or Stage A’s fixed corpus origin.

4. **Material — §1 Q4, §5 table: several claimed same-kind comparisons have no held-out counterpart.** There is no held-out `rand`-into-`rand` row for judging real or shuffled text placed in `rand`. `doc00` contains all its text positions, so its own text is not a held-out comparator. `d16` has a designated other-document panel but no corresponding designated same-document panel. “Texts into every set” mixes copied construction coordinates with actual extensions, in proportions that change along the ladder. A radius or overlap of that mixture can change solely because the in-sample fraction changed.  
   **Change:** specify a destination-by-cohort table, with separate counts for construction, new positions of seen documents/sequences, and entirely unseen documents/sequences. Use unsampled random positions for a random same-kind comparator, and an independent random sequence if claiming transfer to new sequences. Use a separate natural document or a reduced construction for the single-document comparison, labelling which question it answers. Keep full-text paths for the viewer, but flag copied points and do not mix them into held-out scores. Save the shuffle’s source-position map so an original occurrence can be paired with its shuffled version.

5. **Material — §§5–6, lines 72–92: “where it lands” and the radius ratio need operational definitions.** `rows` uses the frozen training radii and Sinkhorn scales, then row-normalises; it does not redo Sinkhorn with the point added. `joint_forward` solves one tridiagonal system **across all layers per mode** (`/data/code/DepthEmbedding/invert/dm.py:253–269`). A placed coordinate at layer 0 can therefore change when only later activations change. Every finite query gets neighbours, even far outside support. Two point clouds can have equal RMS radius but lie in different directions; a translated, tightly packed cloud can have the same radius as a dispersed one.  
   **Change:** define the ratio explicitly as the square root of the ratio of mean squared *raw* coordinates over the declared tokens and modes, and specify layer weighting for the overall number. This is radius about the **construction origin**, not centred spread or probability of belonging. Alongside it show a location or paired-displacement statistic and a support diagnostic such as nearest construction cosine or realised row width, against the same-kind held-out reference. Record \(\sigma_{\min}(T_k)\), condition number and solve residual; define when a mode or a ratio with a tiny denominator is withheld. For shuffled-versus-original checks, equality for matching IDs at layer 0 applies to the **local source row** under identical masking, not necessarily to the joint coordinate.

6. **Material — §§4, 6, 8: single modes and rank-\(k\) overlaps can be unstable in precisely the plots being proposed.** Mean-zero joint columns are orthonormal over the *stack*, not at each layer or on a placed cohort. After centring a restricted panel, columns can be zero, dependent, or numerically negligible. An ordinary QR can complete them with arbitrary directions and return an apparently valid overlap. The weakest relative singular value I found among the pilot Pythia 64-column layer slices was about \(1.3\times10^{-5}\), already at \(t^*\). Near-tied eigenmodes can also rotate, changing extreme tokens, individual support and apparent axes without changing the space. A small eigensolver residual certifies an eigenpair, not its rank in the spectrum.  
   **Change:** define per-layer versus stacked centring, use rank-revealing SVD on unquantised coordinates, report effective ranks/occupancy, and mark unsupported fixed-\(k\) comparisons unavailable rather than silently completing a basis. Compute guard modes beyond 64 and report cutoff gaps at 8, 16 and 64; report tied bands as spaces. For side-by-side plots either label axes as unmatched or use a single clearly labelled post-hoc rotation across depths with its residual. Define the k-means inputs, rank, \(k\), and seed, or omit that colour option; it is a fit, not a discovered partition.

7. **Material — §6, lines 86–89, and §4, lines 67–68: “lives on tokens” conflates concentration over depth with concentration over tokens.** `/data/tm_acts/pilot/depthpilot.py:58–69` computes participation over all \(17N\) **layer–token pairs**. A mode uniform over all tokens but present at only one layer would score \(1/17\), even though it uses every token. Also, the reported adjacent-layer cosines do not justify “depth shows little”: the median layer-0-to-16 cosine across the 64 saved modes was **0.048 in Pythia and 0.090 in OLMo**, despite mean adjacent cosines around 0.96.  
   **Change:** name the pilot statistic for what it measures. For token support use \(p_i=\sum_l f_l(i)^2\) and \(1/(N\sum_i p_i^2)\); keep depth weights separately. Define document-explained variance as within-layer between-document sums of squares divided by total within-layer sums of squares, and mark one-document contrasts inapplicable. Show endpoint or depth-lag similarity as well as adjacent similarity; flag negligible slices before interpreting cosines or extreme tokens.

8. **Material — §7.2 and §8 audit, lines 102–104, 128–131: the refit is a stability comparison, and subspace overlap alone cannot audit the release.** Adding 256 tokens changes the mean, graph, scalings, coupling and unit-norm eigenvector amplitudes. The pilot fits a rotation on shared rows and reports \(1-\mathrm{SSE}/\sum b^2\), not held-out centred variance explained (`/data/tm_acts/pilot/depthpilot.py:46–49,82–94`). The shared score is on the rotation-fitting rows, not a known answer or upper bound. Separately, multiplying all released placement coordinates by ten leaves the proposed audit overlap unchanged while making the radius ten times larger. Recomputing a chart from those same bad coordinates would still pass.  
   **Change:** freeze the split, \(k\), fit rows, shared rotation across layers, scale convention and formula. Call this *agreement with a refit*. For the independent audit check keyed token identities, sampled raw-to-row weights, eigenvalues/order and gaps, orthogonality/residuals, and aligned **coordinate-amplitude** errors on construction and held-out rows. Cover a synthetic-input placement as well as natural text. Preserve float64 diagnostics for tight checks; separately verify int8 decoding, scales, saturation, and the displayed facts against the shipped inventory.

9. **Material — §§4, 9: the pilot justification misreports the selected-rule results.** In `/data/tm_acts/pilot/docmodes2_pythia.log`, `D32 set_mean knn15` has **9** document-dominated modes at layer 16 and **5** at layer 12, not “at most 4.” The OLMo layer-16 count of 13 is correct. The `knn15` localisation range 0–4 holds for the four nonzero layers in the second pilot, not for layer 0: `/data/tm_acts/pilot/docmodes.json` gives 9, 13 and 9 for `D01`, `D08`, `D32` there. The 5–23 range is for the Shannon rule on the second pilot’s grid, not all perplexity rules. The raw-origin pilot did **not** run raw `knn15`, so it cannot isolate the effect of centring under the chosen graph.  
   **Change:** generate a small table from the JSON keyed by model, layers, rule, origin and threshold. Mark missing cells and do not attribute a difference to a factor that was not crossed. The 84–92 s solves, rounded held/refit scores and 0.69–0.77 other-document radii are supported. I also reproduced 0.763 stacked top-16 overlap and 0.710 at layer 0 from the saved arrays; identify the latter as a joint slice and give the permutation convention for the reference value.

10. **Material — §8, lines 117–119: the stated site payload does not fit as specified.** The “Texts” panel alone requires \(11\times 4\times2047\times2\times3\times17\times32=\mathbf{293,981,952}\) int8 coordinate bytes before metadata or any other panel. This counts each requested text coordinate once per setting, whether stored as construction or placement; referring to construction coordinates instead of duplicating them does not remove this lower bound on the combined data. Loading on demand reduces initial download, not total published bytes.  
    **Change:** distinguish stored size, compressed transfer size and initial load. Specify an actual reduced viewer tier—fewer axes, fewer placement/coupling combinations, or selected paths—with the full numerical facts retained off-site. Measure the built inventory and enforce the stated cap before publication.

11. **Material — §10: the pilot supports a solve-time estimate, not a 3 GB end-to-end memory bound.** At the observed rate, 66 first solves alone extrapolate to about **92–101 minutes**, so 2–3 hours is not absurd for solves. But the timed row construction used two panels of only 256 tokens. Just one float64 row tensor for all 8,188 text positions is **2.12 GiB**. The pilot builds per-layer lists and then stacks them, temporarily duplicating large arrays; `joint_modes` also forms full lazy matrices and site eigenvectors and recalibrates on each call. Refit, identity controls, audit, packing and I/O are not in the 84–92 seconds. A naïve dense independent audit of the \(34{,}816\)-dimensional joint operator needs over 9 GiB for one matrix.  
    **Change:** specify bounded placement blocks, reuse each block’s rows across strengths, cache site spectra/calibration once per model/set, and use a structured independent audit rather than the dense joint matrix at full size. Before the full run, measure peak RSS, wall time and output bytes on the largest complete placement path at the intended thread count, with a stop/resume rule for the shared VPS.

12. **Material — §2 and §10: the random generator, extraction adapter and hard spend cap are not yet reproducible.** The 32 contexts contain **9,490 distinct observed IDs**. Sampling uniformly over those types without replacement *per sequence*, sampling 8,188 types once across all four sequences, and sampling the permitted vocabulary range are different experiments. None matches natural unigram frequencies; a natural-versus-random contrast changes repetition and composition as well as order. Stage A’s CLI rejects anything but 36 canonical contexts and writes to Stage A paths (`/data/code/TransformerModes/exp1/extract.py:177–181,242–255`). Its ledger records 36-context **L4** jobs of 58.8 s and 283.5 s, not a measured eight-context A10G run. A separate ledger filename alone does not enforce a cap.  
    **Change:** specify the sorted ID population, replacement scope, RNG, exact arrays and hashes; preserve Stage A’s token-string and separator assertions. Describe shuffled text as the composition-preserving order contrast. Use a narrow adapter around the pinned forward path and an isolated output root. Budget both units’ worst-case runtime, CPU/RAM, startup and any retries **before** launch, rejecting reservations over $1. Treat that sum as a proposed cap requiring the owner’s go-ahead, not authorization from this spec.

13. **Minor — §§4, 8: pin the implementation and avoid an experiment-number collision.** `/data/code/DepthEmbedding/invert/dm.py` has no commit ID in its current header. The sibling checkout I inspected was `6c11546b4793a1ae9193561979f40476aad42fc6`. `/data/code/TransformerModes/SPEC_B.md` already reserves **E4** for a different question. Record the source commit and hash in the copy manifest, allocate a noncolliding experiment ID, and define \(t^*\) in the primer as an **eigenvalue crossing**, not an optimum or an equality of the two energy terms.

**Checks and limits.** I read the specs, implementation, pilot scripts and saved outputs; recalculated selected pilot measures from the NPZs; and ran small local NumPy checks, including masked replay. I did not rerun the full eigensolves or tests, extract synthetic contexts, benchmark a full placement/audit, measure a finished site, or verify current Modal pricing. No files were modified and nothing ran on Modal. The payload calculation assumes the stated 32 int8 coordinates at every layer for every listed text and setting; if 150 MB means compressed transfer rather than published data, that is a different limit and needs a build measurement.
