### Material findings in v2

**1. Blocking — P9 is sound, but P10 and K6 ask for an undefined cross-model increment.** §§5, 7–9, lines 146–155, 188–203, 218–229, 266–275.

At fixed \(c_\ell>0,t\), the difference in the two depth operators is exactly the positive-semidefinite block diagonal in P9. By min–max, every ordered eigenvalue **does not decrease**, and the sum defining \(E\) is nonnegative. Strict rise is not guaranteed. Fixing the partner's scales and coupling is the right choice for this comparison: it isolates the added roughness instead of allowing a refit to absorb it. The product is not necessarily at *its own* balance point; reporting its own fitted values is sufficient. A small \(E\) relative to shuffles means **the prediction constraint is cheaper**, not that the eigenvectors moved more. I would use that wording in the opening question too.

For P10, write \(U=I_L\otimes\Pi\). If all layer walks commute with \(\Pi\), then \(L^\Pi=U L U^\top\). This proves equal spectra and equivariance of **whole spectral projectors**, not of an arbitrary selection from a tied eigenspace. It gives equality between correct and wrong costs, **not** \(E=0\). For cross-model agreement both models must satisfy the commutation condition for the same permutation.

The mandatory flat-layer fixture is exactly where the qualification matters. On mean-zero functions \(A_\ell=aI\), \(c_\ell=1-a^2\), and
\[
L_{\rm past}=I+t(D_{\rm path}\otimes I).
\]
Its lowest eigenvalue has multiplicity \(N-1\). **No past-only cutoff from 8 to 16 is resolved.** Under the spec's own cutoff rule there is no past-only 16-mode picture, hence no defined \(\Delta\) for K6. Accepting a solver's arbitrary 16 columns instead would defeat the fixture.

The same conflict can occur on real data: §7 permits smaller \(K\) or no space, while §8.3 requires 16 in both models and all wrong-pairing runs. Dropping only unresolved shuffles would bias the reference.

**Change:** keep P9 with “does not decrease.” Make K6 test site spectra, \(E=E_\Pi\), and equivariance of the *product* projector when isolated; explicitly require the flat past-only space and \(\Delta\) to be labelled **unresolved**. For the real \(\Delta\) test the simplest rule is fixed \(K=16\), with the test withheld if any required boundary is unresolved; do not remove individual shuffles. \(E\) needs no such exclusion: a sum of the lowest 16 eigenvalues is well defined even at a tie.

**2. Blocking — the count threshold has the wrong sign on an allowed exact null.** §§5, 8.1, 10, lines 150, 207–216, 283–289.

Take \(S_F\) to be the complete graph and \(A_\ell\) invertible on the mean-zero sector. By P5 all nonconstant product eigenvalues are negative. Every permutation leaves \(S_F\) unchanged, so the true and null maxima are the **same** \(\lambda<0\). But the stated test accepts \(\lambda>(1+10^{-9})\lambda\). Thus \(n_\ell\ge1\) when exactly nothing changed.

Also, projecting both sides of a matrix-free operator onto mean-zero vectors while running Lanczos in ambient \(N\)-space leaves an artificial constant eigenvalue zero. It can beat every genuine negative eigenvalue—the very defect the new `site_scales_mz` wrapper correctly fixes elsewhere.

**Change:** use a nonnegative additive tie margin, e.g. \(\lambda_{\rm obs}>\lambda_{\rm null}+\epsilon_{\rm abs}+\epsilon_{\rm rel}|\lambda_{\rm null}|\), sized against eigenvalue residuals. Solve in the actual \(N-1\) sector or move the constant strictly below the spectrum; request the **largest algebraic**, not largest-magnitude, eigenvalue. Run the complete-prediction fixture through the *entire* observed and null paths and require zero. Use a numerical-tie convention in the \(E,\Delta\) permutation ranks as well, so roundoff cannot make an analytically invariant statistic look exceptional.

**3. Blocking for a family-level finding — the permutation design is good, but six prespecified tests do not constitute a multiplicity rule.** §§1, 6, 8, lines 43–52, 174–181, 207–229.

The free randomisation tests are valid against the null that the **paired two-model prediction records** are exchangeable relative to the fixed layer records. The token-preserving versions require joint exchangeability **within exact cut-token groups**. Common permutations across layers and models are correct; independently shuffling the models would destroy the very prediction–prediction agreement Q2b should preserve. These are reference hypotheses, not exchangeability guaranteed merely by drawing independent documents. In particular, subset and formatting associations can remain beyond the cut token.

The one-sided \(+1\) p-values are appropriate under those hypotheses, with ties handled conservatively. But if any of six \(p<.05\) values can furnish the headline, there is no family-level .05 control. With 50 shuffles the smallest p-value is \(1/51=.0196\); **none** can clear the first Holm threshold of \(.05/6=.00833\). The 0.27 figure is an expected count for the 54 **free-shuffle full-sample** cells, not a familywise error rate for the study or a calculation including token and half-sample cells.

The token permutation is well defined with singletons. Its power may be poor: if there are \(d\) distinct cut-token IDs among \(N\) chunks, the expected fraction actually moved under a uniform within-group shuffle is \((N-d)/N\), not the printed fraction merely *eligible* to move. A size-two group remains intact half the time. Fixed rows do not invalidate a p-value, but weak coverage cannot support “the token explains it.”

**Change:** cheapest is to designate **one primary test** with its direction and decision threshold, and print the other five as secondary raw reference p-values with no “any success” claim. Alternatively specify a valid family procedure and fund enough permutations. Print actual moved fraction, group-size distribution and coverage for each sample; predeclare an **uninformative token reference** outcome for inadequate movement. Say “beyond exact cut-token identity on this sample,” not “beyond textual nuisance.” A positive \(\Delta\) and a \(\Delta\) merely *less negative than shuffled* are also different outcomes.

**4. Should-fix before using the HMM as a spend gate — distinguish a bad fixture, a failed intended property, and a code failure.** §9–10, lines 242–289.

The revised gate tests a materially better question. Scoring an orthonormal basis of *within-symbol* belief removes the first-round 80%-explained shortcut. K0 is a sensible feasibility check. But neither its \(0.3/0.5\) split nor the donor-capture ceilings follow from the HMM or from the product theorem. K2, K4 and K5 in particular demand small **absolute** capture even though a lazy layer can transmit donor-prediction modes without evidence of correct pairing. K6 demonstrates why that can happen. Failure is meaningful if rejection of that leakage is an owner's requirement; it is **not** necessarily evidence the operator or implementation is wrong. A single randomly chosen wrong pairing in K4 is a fragile basis for such a conclusion. The unrelated-future control should also say explicitly which synthetic records and RNG streams are held fixed.

**Change:** run analytic and tie fixtures first, then K0 alone, before the expensive null loops. **If K0 fails, stop with “fixture uninformative”; do not spend on extraction, do not call it a negative result, and do not silently adjust the variance and retry.** Any redesign gets a version, fresh confirmatory seeds and a new gate-only authorisation. For K1–K5 either explicitly keep the absolute thresholds as suitability requirements, or gate on the true-pairing gain over wrong pairings and report raw donor leakage separately. Freeze that choice now. Test permutation calibration by exhaustive ranking on a tiny fixture rather than demanding “about 1 in 201” in 400 seeds: even ideal independent calibrated trials yield **zero** exceedances about 14% of the time.

**5. Should-fix — fixed PR64 is defensible; “dense, therefore connected and informative” is not.** §§3–5, 7, lines 102–129, 165–166, 188–203.

The explicit tie branch sets off-set weights to **zero**, contradicting line 119. With exactly 64 exact minima, the target is attained in the limiting distribution; with 100 it is unattainable. With merely *near*-equal minima, uniform mass on the \(10^{-12}\) set is an explicit tie convention, not literally the \(\beta\to\infty\) limit of the original distances. Untied rows can also underflow. Disconnected walks may have a non-unique eigenvalue-1 basis, so a full `eigh` need not return one eigenvector identifiable as the constant by the proposed dot-product threshold. `/home/ubuntu/code/TransformerModes/exp1/kernels.py:39–92` has no tolerance-set branch; the wrapper is a modification to the row rule, not simply a call to the unchanged oracle.

Stage A's flat rows are a reason to measure \(\tau_q,\tau_S\) and the spectrum, **not** a reason to introduce another switch. Nor does small variation in the raw predictions imply \(n_\ell=0\): adaptive bandwidth can magnify arbitrarily small distance variation.

**Change:** retain PR64 and no floor. Test the actual post-tie support, row residual, component count and spectral gap; distinguish a disconnected descriptive layer-0 reference from an inadmissible primary balance solve. State what gets withheld in each case. Report raw Hellinger-distance scale beside final-walk tails. If the reference is broad or unresolved, present that result rather than interpreting the nearest eight as where most walk mass goes.

**6. Blocking for a blanket $8 approval — the hard cap can prevent overspend without buying a complete experiment.** §§7–9, 12–13, lines 196–198, 207–216, 352–400.

Three arithmetic checks matter:

- The prescribed completion holds are **$4.212**: U4 at \(43\) minutes and two U5 attempts at \(78\) minutes each, all at $1.27/h. The table's expected pre-completion work through U3 totals **$3.82** with the paid HMM. That is already **$8.032 before the separate coordinator/startup line**. This does not prove every reduced tier impossible; it does show why the $5.60 expected bill is not a tier-1 admission calculation.
- Per model I count **7,200 full-size null eigensolves**: \((27\text{ free}+9\text{ token})\times200\), plus **7,200 half-size**: \(2\text{ halves}\times9\text{ sites}\times2\text{ references}\times200\). Both models total **28,800 leading-eigenvalue jobs**, separate from the through-depth solves and HMM. The U2 “54 counts” does not reconcile these. The pilot times one kind of site and one 200-null batch, not the spectrum-dependent iteration spread, both half workloads, full 64-lag Gram, or production Hellinger calculation. In particular a literal broadcast of the squared Hellinger differences has shape \(2048\times2048\times50304\), about **1.69 TB** in float64. It must be blocked or computed by a verified alternative. The production dense solver also requires **SciPy**, notwithstanding “CPU with numpy.”
- **The independent ordering audit is valid in principle.** The constant-slice sector is invariant; removing **one copy** of each of its eight known eigenvalues leaves precisely the spatial multiset, including at a past-only balance collision. Each dense matrix is **2 GiB**, not an obvious problem for a serial 64 GiB worker. Time is different: four `eigvalsh` calls (two models, product and partner) entail roughly \(2.35\times10^{13}\) floating-point operations for dense symmetric tridiagonal reduction alone, before rebuilding walks, audit nulls, independent calibration or I/O. The pilot does **not** time even one of those calls. I cannot say the 75-minute limit will fail; it is not supported by the given anchor.

**Change:** publish the admission arithmetic per tier: spent, outstanding hard reservations including startups, primary work remaining, completion/retry holds, and worst-case payable on stop. Pilot one real ordering check, reuse its output, and use measured stage-specific costs, including full/half nulls and the slow lag/audit path. If the dense check dominates, an independent inertia count at a guarded low-spectrum boundary using the block-tridiagonal operator, together with residuals and the small full-spectrum fixture, is a legitimate alternative. For dense subtraction require a numerical match to **each** predicted temporal eigenvalue, preserve multiplicities, and report the maximum mismatch; do not drop every value within a tolerance band.

Tier 2 promises no exploratory through-depth wrong pairings while the site promises a wrong-pairing reference wherever such a picture appears. Tier 3 omits two owner-requested summaries but the audit still requires their production distance rows. Specify the exact outputs, references, and audit for each tier; suppress unsupported panels rather than implying they were verified. Tier 3 is a **primary-comparison-only result**, not completion of the three-summary request, unless the owner explicitly accepts it.
