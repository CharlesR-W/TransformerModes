### Disposition of the 13 original findings

1. **Partly closed — material — §§4–5, 7.2, lines 74–91, 100–106, 150–153.** The radius rule **is correct in exact arithmetic**. Mask self *before* finding the 15th distance, give the masked entry zero weight, and use the same distances in both paths. Then masked query \(i\) selects exactly the fitted undirected edges of \(i\). Row-normalising \(W_{ij}v_j\) recovers \(v_iW_{ij}v_j=P_{ij}\), up to the Sinkhorn residual. This is a real improvement over v1.

   It is **not** sufficient to use float64 and `<=` in two independent matrix multiplications. On the first 512 text positions of Pythia `c00`, using the **zero origin and v2 radius rule**, I recomputed the masked rows from activations. With a 512-query block, all rows passed at layers 0 and 8; maximum total-variation errors were below \(5\times10^{-12}\). With seven-query blocks, **16 rows failed at layer 0** (worst 0.214), and **22 failed at layer 8** (worst 0.122). A dot product rounded to the opposite side of a stored reverse radius can flip a unit-weight edge to \(10^{-6}\). This does not require repeated IDs.

   **Change:** define a reproducible distance calculation for construction and query blocks, including normalisation, accumulation, symmetry and mask order. One efficient option is a fast matrix multiply with a canonical pairwise recalculation for comparisons near either cutoff. Gate it on real rows with different block sizes, a short final block, and reordered queries. Do not relax the TV tolerance to hide genuine edge changes. Describe upstream accurately: its **forward** rule chooses 15, while its out-of-sample **reverse** rule includes all boundary ties.

For `d32` versus `rand`, distinguish the within-model comparison from the two separate cross-model scores in line 145.

5. **Partly closed — material — §§5–6, lines 98–106, 128–133.** Frozen scalings, cross-depth placement, radius about the construction origin, centre offset and nearest-token support are now described correctly. The missing numerical diagnostic is still the **smallest singular value** of the per-mode tridiagonal matrix \(T_k\), or equivalently the norm of its inverse. A small residual is compatible with strongly amplified source errors; the condition number alone measures relative, not absolute, sensitivity.  
   **Change:** record \(\sigma_{\min}(T_k)\) once per embedding/strength/mode, define the residual normalisation, and specify a warn/withhold rule for singular or unreliable extensions. Define whether the pooled centre offset averages layer centroids first or takes their RMS magnitudes—oppositely directed shifts cancel in the former. 6. **Partly closed — material — §§4, 6, 8, lines 84–85, 135–139, 174–189.** Guard modes, rank-revealing SVD, unmatched axes and removal of unspecified k-means address much of v1.    **Change:** for fixed-\(k\) agreement require both ranks \(k\), or use a stated rank-penalising measure and show both ranks; retain the present formula only if labelled containment. 7. **Closed — §§6, 9, lines 120–126, 209–214.** The new token-support statistic correctly marginalises over depth. 8. **Partly closed — material — §§7.3, 8, lines 154–158, 170–189.** The refit is now a properly labelled comparison, with 256 held-out rows, a specified fit population, \(k\) values and score. An ordered list of small-residual eigenpairs can still skip a mode; independent solvers can return rotated bases for a valid repeated band. A sign-only amplitude test can therefore both miss an ordering error and reject a correct solve.  
   **Change:** specify tolerances, an independent low-mode count at scored cutoffs or an equivalent ordering check, and a band-level orthogonal alignment **without rescaling**, fitted only on shared construction coordinates and applied unchanged to held-out rows. `/data/code/TransformerModes/SPEC_B.md:433–439` gives a structured counting precedent. Retain enough inputs and diagnostics to audit Sinkhorn, solves and quoted float64 facts; specify looser, separate tolerances for float32 release coordinates and int8 display data.

### Additional material problems

- **Material — §§4, 6, lines 71–77, 88–96, 128–133: the final graph is not always a 15-neighbour graph.** Under the inclusive radius rule, if an ID occurs \(m\) times at layer 0, a position can acquire all \(m-1\) identical-ID neighbours, not just 15. This happens at meaningful scale: `doc01` has an ID occurring **196** times. The pilot’s old graph and the final graph can therefore have substantially different degrees, weights and spectra exactly where the ID comparison matters most. **Change:** call it a *15th-neighbour-radius union graph*. Record realised degree and fraction tied at the cutoff by layer/set, Sinkhorn row/column residuals, and an explicit failure threshold. Name both departures from the image method: zero rather than probe-mean origin, and inclusive ties. An old-rule pilot remains useful if labelled as such.

- **Material — §6 Q2/Q4 and §7.4, lines 141–145, 159–164: label precisely what a comparison controls for.** In a single-document destination, a whole-panel within-ID permutation can exchange copied and extended occurrences. An excess over that reference may then include agreement about **construction membership**. And “`ov_k` between models for … `rand` against … `d32`” does not uniquely identify the two matrix arguments: there are two models and two destinations. **Change:** write each comparison as an explicit pair, e.g. `ov(Pythia_rand, OLMo_rand)` beside `ov(Pythia_d32, OLMo_d32)` for cross-model agreement, versus `ov(model_rand, model_d32)` for within-model transfer. For single-document panel controls, report the genuinely held-out cohort separately or permute within `(ID, membership)` and give its movable fraction.
