## The operator and what it measures

**1. Blocking — a prediction-shaped low space is not necessarily shared structure.** §5, lines 140–179; §8, lines 277–288; E5–E6, lines 347–349.

Take a layer with *no preferred pair of chunks*: \(S_\ell=(\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top-I)/(N-1)\). On mean-zero functions its lazy walk is \(A_\ell=aI\), where \(a=(N-2)/(2(N-1))\). Consequently
\[
M_\ell=a^2S_F .
\]
**Every prediction eigenvector is recovered exactly**, irrespective of what the layer represents. Every relabelling also has exactly the same eigenvalues, so the strict count is correctly **zero** in exact arithmetic. The distinction matters: the count can say “no evidence of pairing” while the mode card shows a striking prediction pattern. If all sites are like this, coupling them through depth does not make that pattern evidence about the layers.

There is a sound reason to use a lazy *layer* walk: it prevents a nearly alternating layer eigenvector with eigenvalue near \(-1\) from becoming cheap merely because the layer step is taken twice. There is also a sound reason to prefer a non-lazy *prediction* walk: making it lazy introduces an explicit \(A_\ell^2/2\) past-only term. But neither choice makes the product an intersection projector. For a uniformly relabelled zero-diagonal \(S_F\), the **expected matrix** on mean-zero functions is \(-A_\ell^2/(N-1)\); an individual null matrix can have large, structured eigenvectors. And dividing gaps by their mean does not generally undo halving eigenvalues: \(1-\lambda/2\) is not a constant multiple of \(1-\lambda\). Likewise, an uninformative prediction made of *identical probability rows* does not automatically produce a complete graph under the stated graph builders; it produces ties.

**Change:** keep the operator and its exact energy identity. Describe it as *charging prediction roughness after layer smoothing*. Add the flat-layer counterexample as a mandatory fixture. Show prediction-only capture/alignment and a wrong-pairing reference for the headline through-depth result; never infer shared structure from a product mode's appearance or belief capture alone. Separate “the operator returned 16 modes” from “there is evidence those modes depend on the correct pairing.”

**What checks out:** P1–P5 as algebraic statements, including the bounds on the positive ordered eigenvalues in P3. Symmetry, nonnegativity and stochasticity follow by multiplication; \(I-M=(I-A^2)+A(I-S_F)A\) proves the energy identity and \(M\preceq A^2\). Equality at eigenvalue 1 requires \(Af=f\) and \(S_Ff=f\), giving the common-component claim. These facts hold for the unscaled site matrices. The paired *through-depth* runs also change their fitted \(c_\ell\) and \(t^*\); report these rather than suggesting the displayed difference is measured at fixed coupling.

**2. Should-fix — the CCA result is correct; the stated Hankel conclusion is not.** §3, lines 117–123; §5, lines 160–167, 183–188.

For orthonormal bases \(U,V\) of the centred feature spans, the nonzero spectrum of \(P_UP_VP_U\) is \(\sigma_i(U^\top V)^2\): exactly the squared sample canonical correlations. Their nonzero count is the rank of the **chosen sample cross-covariance**. It is not generally the dimension of a linear Gaussian process's minimal state. For example, \(y_t=e_t+a e_{t-2}\) with white Gaussian \(e_t\) has zero covariance between \(y_t\) and \(y_{t+1}\), but the two-past/two-future covariance has rank two for \(a\ne0\). Conversely, finite-sample noise can give full sample rank. A realization-rank statement needs a population past–future Hankel operator, sufficiently rich horizons, and minimality conditions. Canonical correlations use whitened covariance, not arbitrary unweighted Hankel singular values.

**Change:** end the theorem at sample CCA. Put the realization result in a conditional motivation paragraph. Call the actual model view a **one-step predictive distribution**, not a measured Hankel rank or a sufficient predictive state for continuations. The final head is deterministic, but a deterministic norm–head–softmax map need not preserve neighbourhoods, and \(n_{16}\) is not a mathematical ceiling on earlier counts. Call it an endpoint reference.

## The known-answer gate

**3. Blocking — the gate can fail on legitimate prediction ties and pass on the current symbol.** §3, lines 110–115; §5, line 174; §8, lines 249–288.

I ran only the tiny three-state filter, not the gate. With the specified transition/emission probabilities, 1,024 independent length-192 sequences and seeds 60–62, I found **16,470, 16,290 and 16,074 unordered pairs** respectively with squared Hellinger distance below \(10^{-9}\). I computed it as \(\tfrac12\|\sqrt p-\sqrt q\|^2\), avoiding subtraction cancellation. There were even bit-identical filtered rows. Long common suffixes legitimately give nearly identical posteriors. If the duplicate stop applies to predictions, the production-path gate stops for the wrong reason. If it applies only to hidden states, the spec has no adequate prediction-tie rule. Distinct nodes can also tie at the 15th-neighbour boundary without being duplicates.

In the same draws, the **three current-symbol indicators alone explained 78.5%, 81.7% and 80.3%** of total centred belief variance. The exact numbers depend on RNG draw order; the shortcut does not. Belief and current symbol are planted and scrambled together. Two independent centred symbol indicators can supply two directions, and a token-dominated picture can approach the 80% target without recovering the posterior differences between histories ending in the same symbol. The negative control also leaves a hole: measuring capture of the *true* belief after substituting unrelated predictions does not test whether the operator simply displays the *donor's* belief. K3 bounds capture of the scrambled belief at the scrambled site, not the true belief imported there from the prediction or adjacent sites. Relative site units and path coupling do not imply K4's strict occupancy ordering.

**Change:** distinguish duplicate **documents**, identical feature vectors, numerical roundoff and graph-boundary ties. Allow legitimate repeated predictions under a declared permutation-equivariant tie policy; report unattainable PR targets as such, not as failed HMM science. Add a **symbol-only** site or a belief shuffle *within current symbol*. Gate positive recovery on the within-symbol belief component as a **readout**, against symbol-only, prediction-only and wrong-pairing controls. In the negative control score both recipient and donor belief. Gate local recovery locally; report depth leakage and occupancy separately rather than requiring a strict ordering as a test of implementation. Specify the capture denominator and numerical slice-rank tolerance: projecting belief onto the span of a tiny slice discards how little energy that slice carries. Freeze the revised gate before running it; do not tune thresholds against these three seeds.

**4. Blocking for the one-step versus two-step claim — the four-state source has the wrong known answer.** §8, lines 254–257, 275–288.

With the stated **move, then emit** convention, the vector of probabilities of next emitting 0, starting from A, B, C, D, is
\[
T(0.5,0.95,0.5,0.05)^\top=(0.905,\ 0.545,\ 0.095,\ 0.455)^\top.
\]
A and C are strongly distinguished in one step, not seen only through their sum. I get rank four for the four-state-by-four-ordered-two-symbol probability matrix. There *is* a horizon effect, but not the one described. Also, a belief contrast linearly residualised against one-step probability need not be independent of nonlinear functions of that probability on the filtered-belief distribution.

**Change:** supply the emission-labelled transition matrices and exact one- and two-step observation matrices as fixtures. Choose two explicit beliefs with identical one-step distributions but different two-step distributions, or redesign the source so the intended pair is one-step indistinguishable by construction. Specify whether the two-step feature is the **joint** distribution of ordered symbol pairs or concatenated conditional distributions; they are different geometries. I would defer this source rather than let an incorrect illustration hold up the one-step experiment.

## Inference on real chunks

**5. Blocking for the E4 headline — the shuffle tests a narrower null than “how many directions the layer shares.”** §§1, 5, 7, lines 47–53, 161–167, 213–220; E4, line 347.

For **one prespecified cell**, if pairing is exchangeable and the statistic has no ties, the probability that its leading eigenvalue beats all 20 shuffled maxima is indeed \(1/21\) (at most that with strict comparison and ties). That calibrates the event *any eigenvalue clears this threshold under complete random pairing*. It does not identify each counted direction as a distinct true effect, test a memory-minus-cut difference, or calibrate selecting the most interesting depth. There are **104 full-sample cells** including both readings and models: under a continuous complete null one expects about five cells to light up, without needing them to be independent. Each cell has a different null threshold. Counts need not go down on halves, nor go up monotonically with better prediction information. A line at one is not the expected null count.

**Change:** name the number literally: “eigenvalues exceeding the largest of 20 wrong-pairing maxima, at this graph resolution.” Show the observed spectrum and threshold for the primary comparison. Use common permutations across sites/comparisons for a prespecified **paired contrast** or depth-curve statistic; calibrate the family if making a peak or “memory raises” claim. Otherwise label those patterns exploratory, show the two half contrasts, and allow “indeterminate,” including when both counts saturate at 64. This does not require replacing the construction with fitted probes.

**6. Blocking for an interpretation beyond shared text or output — independent documents fix overlap, not nuisance agreement or circularity.** §2, lines 57–72; §§5–7, lines 165–174, 198–200, 222–247; E5, line 348.

Both models see the **same** cut token, prefix, domain and formatting. Two models that encode only the last token can agree decisively against a free row permutation. Two otherwise unrelated layer geometries can also look more alike when their similar prediction graphs are inserted into both products (finding 1). The free permutation is a fair reference for the literal question “same chunks versus random chunks”; it is not a reference for *agreement beyond these causes*, or for the *increment* on inserting correctly paired predictions. Five separate variance shares do not solve this: they overlap, token columns omit rare IDs, and a top-1 label misses geometry carried by varying probabilities of frequent tokens that never win. Describing a prediction-built basis by predicted token or entropy is useful, not independent validation of predictive function.

Uniform cut position is independent of document length/subset **before** rejection. Whitespace and shared-32-gram rejection depend on the sampled cut. And two copies of the same source text can pass a chunk-only 32-gram test if cut in different places.

**Change:** deduplicate accepted full-text hashes, save source index/hash/cut, and report accepted cut distributions by subset and length. Keep the free-shuffle baseline for total agreement. Add one preregistered nuisance-preserving readout/reference on adequately populated strata, prioritising **exact current token**, reporting movable coverage and the other attributes' joint coverage. For the prediction increment, compare the observed paired change in cross-model alignment with a control that applies the **same wrong chunk permutation to both models' prediction walks**: it retains prediction–prediction agreement while breaking correct layer–prediction pairing. Report prediction-only alignment alongside it. For frequent-token dominance, the per-token contributions \((\sqrt{p_i(v)}-\sqrt{p_j(v)})^2/2\) directly decompose the Hellinger distance. No window, mean subtraction, or label-defined kernel is needed. If these controls are too costly, narrow E5 to *unconditional descriptive agreement*. If claiming actual predictive utility, reserve an outcome not used in construction, such as the observed next token with matched controls; otherwise make no such claim.

**7. Should-fix — the three kernels are distinct, but they do not isolate order or additional memory.** §3, lines 95–115; §§14, 16, lines 445–447, 475–476.

The feature maps are sound: one cut vector; concatenated \(\sqrt{a^s}y_i(s)\); and the mean of the 64 vectors. The stated lost geometric weight is correct. But even at **perfectly uniform** lag weights, end-aligned and averaged comparisons are not nearly equivalent. Reverse a chunk containing orthogonal states: the average stays identical while aligned agreement can go to zero. And a “state at the cut” already represents its prefix. “Order-blind” applies to pooling the **already contextualised states**, not to permuting source tokens and rerunning the model.

**Change:** retain the three requested comparisons; describe them as three summaries, not an order ablation or proof that the model acquired extra memory. Add the reversal fixture and check the **pooled** norm before cosine division. Resolve the centring instruction explicitly: lines 12 and 95–98 say both “no mean removal” and “subtract the sample mean.” If the decision was *no per-document nuisance fix*, say that; if it was literally no mean subtraction in the geometry, the cosine construction must change. No fourth kernel is necessary for a descriptive comparison.

## Graphs, verification, cost and presentation

**8. Should-fix — the graph switch can replace a flat-tail problem with floor-defined modes; a solver helper also mishandles an allowed spectrum.** §§4–6, lines 125–136, 169–179, 190–209.

PR64 targets the **directed conditional** \(q\), not the final Sinkhorn walk. The switch uses the mean tail of \(q\) in four views of one model; the interpreted operator uses \(S\). It neither tests localisation of \(S\) nor detects disconnected components in the unfloored kNN graph. The \(10^{-6}\) floor then makes such a graph technically connected. With nine components, all eight gaps setting \(c_\ell\) can be floor gaps. A tiny eigen-residual does not make the resulting mode a model feature. Adaptive widths also remove absolute distance scale: a set of almost identical but distinguishable predictions need not have a flat walk.

There is a concrete implementation trap in `/home/ubuntu/code/TransformerModes/exp1/spacetime.py:47–59`: `site_spectrum` subtracts the constant projector, **leaving a zero eigenvalue**, then takes the largest eigenvalues. When genuine mean-zero eigenvalues are negative, that zero can be incorrectly selected. The complete-prediction example in P5 supplies exactly this case for invertible \(A\). The core dense solver need not be replaced.

**Change:** compute \(c_\ell\) from the full site decomposition with the verified constant eigenpair **removed**, and test the all-negative mean-zero case. Report conditional and final-walk tails, unfloored component sizes, low-site gaps and the cutoff gap. When near-disconnection matters, repeat the primary result at a second floor or report it as floor-defined. Suppress interpretation of modes or spaces whose boundary cuts an unresolved cluster; merely printing the gap is insufficient. Keep one primary graph rule, not a second full experimental grid.

**9. Blocking for the viewer — pooled PCA of the stated slices has no preferred axes.** §7, lines 222–225; §10.1, lines 318–336.

Stack the eight \(G_\ell\) vertically, as required to do PCA in the common 16 mode coordinates. The columns are the globally orthonormal joint eigenvectors and each slice has mean zero. Therefore the pooled scatter matrix is **exactly** \(\sum_\ell G_\ell^\top G_\ell=I_{16}\). All 16 principal values tie. A plotting library will return two axes, potentially determined by rounding or int8 quantisation, and the moving cloud will look convincing.

**Change:** the simplest honest axes are “joint modes 1 and 2,” *if separated*, explicitly local to the setting. Alternatively choose axes by a prespecified anchor-site SVD with checked gaps and hold them fixed during playback. Suppress the scatter or use a distance/nearest-neighbour view when unresolved. Lead with one fixed chunk, its past-neighbours versus prediction-neighbours, and the raw spectrum versus shuffles. Those are more informative verification aids than four controls and an animation on first load.
