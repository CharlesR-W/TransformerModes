### Canonical replay and cohort rank checks

**Radius replay.** I used the first 512 Pythia `c00` text activations at layers 0 and 8, with the zero origin and stored float64 unit vectors. I took provisional 15th distances from the matrix product, recalculated pairs within \(10^{-9}\) of either provisional endpoint radius using 1-D `numpy.dot`, then took the **final** 15th distances before testing edges. For replay I masked self first and used the frozen construction radii on the reverse side. On 256 reordered queries, block sizes **1, 7 and 256 all gave zero differing edges**. Worst row total-variation error was \(2.0\times10^{-12}\) at layer 0 and \(3.5\times10^{-12}\) at layer 8, consistent with Sinkhorn error. The seven-row case had failed on both layers without canonical recomputation.

I would keep this rule. **Minor implementation clarification:** write those two passes explicitly, use contiguous float64 vectors and the same canonical reduction in both paths. The \(10^{-9}\) guard is comfortably larger than ordinary float64 dot-product rounding error for two unit vectors of width 2,048. It identifies candidates for recomputation; it should **not** be added to the final edge threshold. The evidence supports blocking independence in the pinned implementation, not a promise of bitwise equality across arbitrary BLAS libraries.

**Cohort ranks.** Two nonempty Q3 cohorts are too small for per-layer \(k=64\): the single-document natural **in-set** cohort has 32 rows and centred rank at most **31**; each pair’s natural **new position of a seen sequence** cohort has 64 rows and centred rank at most **63**. The unavailable rule handles both correctly. These bounds do not automatically preclude a rank-64 **stacked** comparison. No other nonempty listed cohort is ruled out by row count alone, though its measured numerical rank can still be deficient. The 992-row Q2 panel has no count problem.

### Material findings to resolve

1. **Material — §§4, 6–8, lines 102–105, 165–170, 225–232: a complete band and a fixed cutoff are not always compatible.** If modes 63–66 form one band, the spec keeps only 63–64 of that band in the release, calls top-64 a space to compare, and asks the audit to align the band. A rotation inside the four-dimensional band can change the selected two-dimensional slice. With an exact tie at 64/65 there is also **no** midpoint at which inertia can certify exactly 64. Computing 72 modes detects many such cases but does not guarantee that a band ends by 72.

   **Change:** keep 1% as a **display convention**, not a proof of degeneracy. If a band straddles 8, 16 or 64, mark the fixed-\(k\) result as cutting a band and withhold its interpretation as a stable subspace, or additionally report a separately labelled complete-band result. Align and audit only complete bands; if the last band reaches the guard limit, extend the solve or mark that band unresolved. Count eigenvalues at **resolved boundaries**, subtracting the known depth-only sector if factorising the full operator. The relevant Stage B precedent is `/data/code/TransformerModes/SPEC_B.md:433–439`.

2. **Material — §8, lines 209–210, 225–235: distinguish a solver check from a storage-precision check.** I rebuilt **one layer** of the original Pythia pilot operator and evaluated the saved float32 modes after promotion to float64. At layer 8 alone, **63 of 64** residual norms exceeded \(10^{-7}\); the median was \(3.76\times10^{-7}\), the maximum \(5.81\times10^{-7}\). The original float64 solve reported a **full-operator maximum** of \(3.93\times10^{-12}\). A full residual norm cannot be smaller than one of its layer contributions. Thus a correct release can fail this audit simply because the coordinates were saved as specified.

   **Change:** apply the \(10^{-7}\) solver-residual gate to **float64 eigenvectors** retained for audited cells; check the float32 release against their actual cast separately. Restore explicit slice-mean-zero and orthonormality checks, which v2 named. For page facts, use exact checks for counts and keys, and quantity-specific absolute-plus-relative tolerances for floating values: a universal relative \(10^{-6}\) is not meaningful at zero or for a tiny solver residual recomputed from rounded vectors. The \(10^{-4}\)-of-band-RMS amplitude check is **not** inherently too tight for float32; retain it for complete bands and state which rows define its RMS. Conversely, a whole int8 step is looser than necessary: specify the rounding rule and compare encoded bytes exactly, or require half a step plus rounding allowance for unsaturated nearest-rounded values.
