Two language models read the same {{fact:x.viewer.texts}} texts once, and every hidden state is stored. For each token we ask where its most similar tokens are, when similarity compares two tokens' recent pasts. Stage A (this site) is that census and the checks around it; Stage B (through-depth modes) waits for its own approval.

Why measure neighbours first

A free diagnostic on a smaller model ({{fact:x.method.pilot.model}}: four Wikipedia articles, two Python standard-library files and two licence texts, {{fact:x.method.pilot.tokens}} tokens each, layers 3, 6 and 9, each token's 64 nearest neighbours, positions from {{fact:x.method.pilot.burn}}) showed that with memory, a token's nearest neighbours are usually nearby in its own text. A through-depth picture built on such neighbours might then be mostly about position within each text; that was not measured. So the experiment first measures where neighbours are, under a small set of accounting choices, before paying for modes.

Memory 2 was added between none and 8 as an interpolation point, not because of a finding.

The stored hidden states are the block outputs (hidden state 16 is before the final normalisation), checked equal to the library's own outputs; a causal check confirms that changing the second half of a text leaves the first half's states unchanged. Texts and tokens

The texts are {{fact:x.method.probe.description}}: from the Pile's test file, documents that are at least 95% ASCII and at least 2,048 tokens long, shuffled with a fixed seed, taking each document's first 2,047 tokens; rejected if mostly whitespace, highly repetitive, sharing a 32-token passage with an accepted text, or from a subset that already has four. {{fact:x.method.probe.texts}} texts from {{fact:x.method.probe.subsets}} subsets were accepted; four more documents make the stitched controls.

Each model reads its own end-of-text token at position 0, which is excluded everywhere. A token is named by its text and position. Positions 1 to {{fact:x.viewer.burn_in}} are burn-in: part of every later token's past, but not analysed.

Similarity

Memory: two pasts aligned at their ends

With $y_u$ a token's hidden state after centring (in the cosine reading also scaled to unit length; $y_u = 0$ before position 1), the similarity of tokens $t$ and $t'$ lays their pasts side by side, aligned at the end, and compares them step by step back:

$$K_a(t,t') = \sum_{s \ge 0} a^s\, \langle y_{t-s},\, y_{t'-s} \rangle = \langle y_t, y_{t'} \rangle + a\, K_a(t-1,\, t'-1).$$

Memory $m$ sets $a = 1 - 1/m$: none ($a = 0$, the current token only), 2 ($a = 0.5$), 8 ($0.875$), 64 ($0.984375$) and the whole prefix ($a = 1$). The cosine reading (the viewer's) normalises twice: each centred token vector to unit length before the sum, so every token counts equally, and then the weighted past, $d = 1 - K_a(t,t')/\sqrt{K_a(t,t)K_a(t',t')}$. The dot reading keeps the centred vectors' lengths, so loud tokens count more, and its distance is $\big[(K_a(t,t) + K_a(t',t'))/2 - K_a(t,t')\big]/\bar K$, with $\bar K$ the mean of $K_a(t,t)$ over the census pool. Three properties matter when reading results: a chance agreement between two pasts persists along the alignment ($K(t,t')$ and $K(t+1,t'+1)$ share most of their terms), so neighbours come in runs; at the whole prefix pasts of different lengths are compared over the shorter one, so similarity depends on position even for structureless text; and the recursion is computed exactly, in float64, never through a closed form that overflows.

Neither centring is the corrected version of the other; they ask different questions and are shown side by side. Both depend only on the token's own text, so two tokens' similarity never depends on which other texts are present.

Text-only adversaries

The same computation on vectors that know only the tokens: a fixed random vector per token id, and each model's embedding output (hidden state 0). Where a model's neighbours overlap these, the neighbourhood could have come from the text alone.

Ties

Two candidates are tied if their distances differ by at most one part in $10^{12}$. The 64 neighbours are every candidate strictly closer than the 64th distance, plus tied candidates at the cutoff taken in (text, position) order. Overlaps between two neighbour sets are computed as the expected overlap under random tie-breaking, $\mathbb{E}|A \cap B| = \sum_j P(j \in A)\, P(j \in B)$, and the screen uses the largest overlap the ties allow, so a candidate cannot pass by tie-breaking.

The census

Every analysed token, every setting (2 models × 2 readings × 2 centrings × 5 memories × 3 masks × 8 layers and 2 adversaries): the shares of its 64 neighbours in other texts, the same text far away and within 64 positions; the median position gap of same-text neighbours; the share with the same current token; overlap with the text-only adversaries and with the same setting at no memory. Chance levels come from the eligible counts. (E1)

The screen on Stage B's own rows. For the sampled suffix queries against every suffix token, the dense walk row Stage B would use, $q(j \mid i) \propto \exp(-\beta_i (d_{ij} - \min_j d_{ij}))$ with $\beta_i$ set so that $1/\sum_j q(j \mid i)^2 = 64$, and its weight on each origin; for the window, the weight the unmasked row put on what the window forbids. Nothing $N \times N$ is stored and no eigenproblem is solved. (E3)

The prediction readout on the query sample at layers 4, 8, 12 and 16: each neighbour's shared prediction mass against a control matched on text, current token and distance band, in six strata. (E2)

Choosing Stage B's construction is fixed in advance: of the 32 candidates, those meeting all four conditions at the same layer for most layers in both models pass; among them the least intervention comes first, then the cosine reading, then the smallest overlap with the text-only adversaries, then the shorter memory. Its local counterpart is the same with no memory. Concordance can add at most one descriptive example.

Controls

Hard checks before any spending (exp1/test_census.py): the streamed kernel against the explicit feature map and a direct double loop; the three masks against eligible sets written by hand; planted exact ties at the cutoff, including 64 taken from 1,024 tied against a set tying all 1,024 (expected overlap 0.0625, largest 1); matched controls never equal to the query, its neighbour or another neighbour, and replayable from their seeds; statistics, bootstrap and the stitched baseline against hand values.

Stitched texts with expected answers: two pairs of documents from different subsets, each cut into alternating segments of 384 to 640 tokens or 48 to 80 tokens. A token's neighbours should come from its own source more often than position alone explains, at little or no memory; at memory 64 less so for the short segments.

Synthetic texts with a planted hidden regime, a position drift or pure noise, run through the same census, so a known structure can be seen as neighbours. Only the {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.synthetic.groups}} settings were run on them ({{fact:x.e1-neighbours.synthetic.runs}} runs), not all four reading and centring groups.

Reference timescales

Beside the memory grid, two descriptive scales per layer: how far back each attention head looks directly (the attention-weighted mean distance, its median over heads), and the lag at which residual similarity within a text falls halfway to its level between texts. Neither is an effective context length. (E1, more figures.)

How to check there is nothing odd going on

An independent audit (exp1/audit_a.py: numpy only, importing nothing from the pipeline) recomputes, for {{fact:x.method.audit.rows}} audit queries, the complete distance rows from the raw activations by the explicit lag sum, then the masks, the 64 neighbours with ties, the screen's walk weights and the removed short-range weight; replays the matched controls from their seeds; recomputes census statistics from the stored lists for a sample of tokens, the readout from its stored contributions and the Stage B selection; and compares 1,000 random pairs per pass with values the census saved before it discarded its distance blocks. The site does not build as a release unless this report is present and passing.

Audit result: {{fact:x.method.audit.passed}}. {{fact:x.method.audit.checks}} checks over {{fact:x.method.audit.compared}} compared values, {{fact:x.method.audit.failed}} failed; models {{fact:x.method.audit.models}}; run with {{fact:x.method.audit.spec_parameters}}.

What the audit does not reproduce, and what it takes on trust
