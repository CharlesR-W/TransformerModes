Do similar tokens agree about what comes next?

Tested: 3,072 fixed query tokens (96 per text: 64 from the last 512 positions and 32 from positions {{fact:x.viewer.first_position}}–1,535), each paired with its 64 nearest neighbours from E1. Predictions are each model's own top-20 next-token probabilities (from its output layer), compared for the query, the neighbour, and a matched stand-in (illustrated below). Lead figure: neighbours from layer {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.layer}}, compared by angle (cosine), one average removed, nothing barred (every analysed token may be a neighbour; layers {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.readout_layers}} were all measured). Five memory settings vary how far back the comparison looks with fading weights (none, about 2, about 8, about 64 tokens, or the whole past equally weighted).

The pre-registered prediction advantage

Rather than looking only at the single top token, the measure fixed before the run (pre-registered) compares the two top-20 next-token lists, with their probabilities. For each pair, we sum the shared probability mass across tokens appearing in both top-20 lists and subtract the stand-in's shared mass. We average those differences within each group over its matched pairs, then give each group with enough stand-ins equal weight. At layer {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.layer}} with one average removed and no tokens barred, this prediction advantage falls from {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.c.mem_none.score}} with no memory to {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.c.mem_whole.score}} for the whole past in {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.name}}, and from {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m2.c.mem_none.score}} to {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m2.c.mem_whole.score}} in {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m2.name}}. The chart below shows this advantage across settings at layer {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.layer}}, with uncertainty bars from 200 resamplings of the {{fact:x.viewer.texts}} texts.

Is the decline just a change in which groups enter the score?

No. Pairs are split into six groups by where the neighbour sits and whether its token is frequent (the neighbour's token occurs at least 20 times among the {{fact:x.viewer.pool_tokens}} analysed tokens) or rare (fewer than 20). Groups where fewer than half the pairs (or fewer than 200 pairs) find a stand-in are left out. Nearby groups never cleared that bar ({{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.near_covered}} of {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.near_strata}}: two nearby groups at each of 4 layers and 5 memories), and rare-token groups often fell short too. At layer {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.layer}} in {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.name}} with no memory, {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.c.mem_none.dropped}} of pairs found a stand-in in a left-out group and {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.c.mem_none.unmatched}} found no stand-in at all. At memory 64, {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.c.mem_64.dropped}} found a stand-in but belonged to a group left out, and {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.c.mem_64.unmatched}} found no stand-in.

Pooling layers {{fact:x.e2-concordance.m1.readout_layers}}, the advantage within each group on its own falls steadily with memory in both models, using all pairs that found a stand-in (including left-out groups). Even so, matched nearby pairs are a selected subset—tokens that repeat close by in the same text. Which groups are counted changes the exact size of the headline number, not its direction.

What this does not show

  • Nothing causal. Whether the model uses these similarities was not tested.
  • What the stand-in does not control. It matches text, token, and distance band only; anything else a neighbour shares with the query, such as its recent past, counts toward the advantage by design.
  • Not that long-memory neighbours are "wrong." One plausible reading, not tested here: memory picks neighbours whose pasts resemble the query's (at memory 64 only {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.local}} of a token's 64 neighbours are the same as with no memory; E1, layer {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layer}}), while the next-token prediction depends mostly on the current state.
  • The no-memory headline excludes rare-token and nearby groups. They did not have enough stand-ins.

Prediction overlapAdd up the probability mass both positions place on the same next tokens, using each position's top 20 predictions.
$o(i,j) = \sum_{v \in T_i \cap T_j} \min\big(p_i(v),\, p_j(v)\big)$		 $T_i$: the model's 20 most likely next tokens after position $i$; $p_i(v)$: full-softmax probability of token $v$ (not renormalised over the top 20) exp1/readout.py: contributions

$j' \sim \text{uniform}(\text{same text, same token, same band})$		 $i$: query; $j$: neighbour; $j'$: stand-in; distance bands (same text only): 1–8, 9–64, 65–512, over 512 tokens; $j'$ is never $i$, $j$, or another neighbour of $i$; seed keyed on model, setting, $i$, and $j$ for exact replay exp1/readout.py: Matcher, keyed_seed

Caveats

  • auditThe first run failed the independent check and was fixed. A 32-bit integer overflow in exp1/readout.py zeroed out prediction overlaps for texts 24–31 and the end of text 23. After switching to 64-bit keys and re-running, the check passed ({{fact:x.method.audit.checks}} checks, {{fact:x.method.audit.failed}} failures). The fix changed only the prediction advantages, which rose. Top-1 rates, the share of pairs with a stand-in, and the E3 pick did not change. See How the numbers were checked.
  • Two-thirds of the query sample sits near the end of each text. Sixty-four of each text's 96 queries come from the last 512 positions, so results are not uniform across positions.
  • Only the top 20 predictions are compared. Any shared probability below the 20th most likely token is invisible.
  • Document resampling does not redraw neighbours or stand-ins. Uncertainty bars show variation across the {{fact:x.viewer.texts}} texts, not across a fresh set of documents.
