Who are a token's nearest neighbours under memory?

Tested: every analysed token of the {{fact:x.viewer.texts}} texts ({{fact:x.viewer.pool_tokens}} tokens, each compared with all the others), both models, the cosine reading, each of the five memories and four accountings, the 64 nearest neighbours of each token. The lead figure is layer 8; the other layers are in the viewer. Not varied: the number of neighbours, the window width, the text sample.

Memory moves a token's nearest neighbours into its own text and next to it: under the corpus mean at layer 8 the share in its own text rises from {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_none.own}} with no memory to {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.own}} at memory 64 in {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}} ({{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m2.c.mem_none.own}} to {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m2.c.mem_64.own}} in {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m2.name}}), and {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.near}} of the 64 sit within 64 positions. The kernel does not do this to any input: random token vectors through the same kernel put {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.random.mem_64.near}} of their neighbours within 64 positions at memory 64, against {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layermean.mem_64.near}} averaged over {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}}'s layers. The window takes away {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.cw.mem_64.removed}} of a row's walk weight at memory 64, and about {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.adv}} of the neighbours are shared with the closer of the two text-only inputs.

Caveats

  • Two shares are set by rule, not found. Under the window the within-64 share is zero; with other texts only, the other-text share is one. The removed walk weight shows how much the window took away.
  • Own-text mean looks ahead. It subtracts each text's average over all its tokens, including later ones, removes topic along with offsets, and makes any two positions of one text slightly anti-correlated.
  • At the whole prefix, position matters by construction: pasts of different lengths are compared over the shorter one, so early and late tokens differ even for structureless text.
  • The texts are a capped sample of the beginnings of long, mostly ASCII Pile test documents, at most four per subset; OLMo's training data are not guaranteed to exclude them.
  • Shares are means over tokens. Texts with many near-duplicate passages can dominate their own rows; the document resampling shows how much the answer moves when texts are swapped.
