Why the whole past behaves differently

When every earlier token counts equally, two histories of different lengths can only be paired up to the shorter one. That length mismatch caps how similar an early token and a late token can ever be, no matter how alike their texts are. Neighbours therefore cluster at similar positions in the text. For each token we took the median distance to its same-text neighbours; at layer {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layer}} in {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}} those medians averaged {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_whole.gap}} tokens. At the whole past, {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.random.mem_whole.near}} of random-vector neighbours and {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_whole.near}} of {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}} neighbours are within 64 tokens. So the comparison itself produces a pull towards similar positions, even without the model's states; the rest of the gap is not explained by this control.

Variations and supporting figures (8 notes, 9 figures)
  • Each text's own average removed: subtracts each document's average over all its tokens, including later ones, and can also remove topic-related differences along with offsets. With each text's own average removed, {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}}'s own-text share goes from {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.o.mem_none.own}} (no memory) to {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.o.mem_64.own}} (memory 64), against {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_none.own}} → {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.own}} with one average removed. Because this average includes later tokens, it is a retrospective contrast rather than a correction.
  • Nearby tokens barred: forbids same-text neighbours within 64 positions, so the nearby share is zero by rule rather than by discovery. At memory 64 in {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}}, this rule removes, on average, {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.cw.mem_64.removed}} of the graph weight (defined in the table below) of queries from each text's last 512 tokens, and still leaves only {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.cw.mem_64.other}} of the 64 nearest in other texts.
  • Own text barred: forces every neighbour into another text by rule, so it is shown only in the same-token comparison below rather than as a location share.
  • Lengths kept (the "dot" setting): ranks neighbours by the distance between the two weighted histories with vector lengths kept, so louder hidden states count more; at the whole past, tokens with the shortest histories end up close to everyone, moving most neighbours into other texts. The preference for short histories also appears with random vectors per token id, so it does not need the model's states.
  • Other layers: averaging across all {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layers}} measured layers gives the same pattern—in {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}}, own-text share rises from {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layermean.mem_none.own}} to {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layermean.mem_64.own}} at memory 64, with {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layermean.mem_64.near}} within 64 tokens.
  • Spliced texts: two documents cut into alternating segments and analysed alone (with the {{fact:x.viewer.texts}} real texts' average removed). In {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}} (mean of the {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.stitched_texts}} spliced texts, layer {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layer}}), same-source share beyond what position alone predicts drops from {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.stitch_pp.mem_none}} percentage points with no memory to {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.stitch_pp.mem_64}} at memory 64 and {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.stitch_pp.mem_whole}} at the whole past ({{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m2.name}}: {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m2.stitch_pp.mem_whole}}). At the whole past, the average same-source share was within a percentage point of what the neighbours' position gaps predict.
  • Synthetic texts: artificial sequences with planted hidden states, slow drift, or pure noise, included as a visual reference rather than a test.
  • Overlap with text-only inputs: text-only inputs are vectors that know only which token is where: a fixed random vector per token id, or the model's input embeddings. At memory 64 in {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.name}} (layer {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.layer}}), {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.adv}} of neighbours are shared with whichever text-only input shares more, while only {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m1.c.mem_64.local}} remain from the model's own no-memory list.

Caveats

  • Shares are averages over tokens, not over pairs. Every analysed token counts once, so every text counts equally. The resampling intervals re-weight the {{fact:x.viewer.texts}} texts but do not recompute anyone's neighbours; they show how much texts differ, not what another corpus would give.
  • The sample is beginnings of long, mostly ASCII documents. At most four documents come from any single Pile source, and {{fact:x.e1-neighbours.m2.name}}'s training data are not guaranteed to exclude these texts.
  • Values shown as 100% or 0% are rounded. At the whole past, own-text shares are more than 99% and other-text shares are under 1% in both models.
  • Text-only inputs have exact ties with no memory. Identical tokens sit at zero distance when memory is none, so ties at the 64th place are filled in text-and-position order (lowest text number first).
  • Barring rules fix shares by definition. Barring nearby tokens forces the within-64 share to zero, and barring the token's own text forces the other-text share to one; neither is a finding.
