Where are a token's nearest neighbours?

Each token of a text is coloured by where its 64 most similar tokens are: in other texts, far away in its own text, or within 64 positions of it. Similarity compares the two tokens' recent pasts, with a memory that sets how far back the comparison reaches. Click a token to see its neighbours as text and check the colour yourself.

Model and layer

Two models with the same shape (16 blocks, width 2,048), each reading the same tokens. Layer picks which block's output is compared: after block 4, 8, 12 or 16 (16 is before the final normalisation).

Memory: two pasts, aligned at their ends

Two tokens are compared through their pasts, laid side by side so that the tokens themselves line up, then each one before, and so on (not by averaging each past). Earlier pairs weigh less, by a factor $a$ per step: $K_a(t,t') = \sum_{s\ge 0} a^s \langle y_{t-s}, y_{t'-s}\rangle$ with $a = 1 - 1/m$ for memory $m$ (none: $a = 0$; whole: $a = 1$, every earlier pair counts equally). Each token's state is centred and scaled to unit length first, and similarity is then the cosine of the two weighted pasts. Lag is paired with lag, so two neighbouring positions of one text share no vectors in the comparison (each token meets the one beside it, never itself); an average-then-dot kernel, the dot product of two running averages, would share 63 of 64 vectors at memory 64 and is not what is computed. Two consequences to keep in mind: a chance match between two pasts carries over to the next pair of tokens, so neighbours come in runs along the diagonal; and with the whole prefix, pasts of different lengths are compared over the shorter one, so position matters even for structureless text.

Accounting: what is subtracted, what is forbidden

Corpus mean subtracts the average state over all texts: which passages does this token resemble, as the model holds it? Own-text mean subtracts its own text's average, topic included, using tokens that come later: a look back over the finished text. The window forbids neighbours within 64 positions in the same text, so orange disappears by rule, not as a finding.

Neighbours and where they come from

A token's neighbours are the 64 most similar of the {{fact:x.viewer.pool_tokens}} analysed tokens of all texts (a stitched text: only its own). Each is in another text, in the same text more than 64 positions away, or within 64. The colour mixes the three by their shares; the card lists the {{fact:x.viewer.shown}} nearest.

Ties

Sometimes several tokens share the 64th place at exactly the same distance (common with no memory, where equal tokens look alike). The list then takes them in (text, position) order and the card marks them ≈; comparisons between lists count tied places fairly rather than by that order.
