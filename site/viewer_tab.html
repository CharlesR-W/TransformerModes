Where are a token's most similar tokens?

Each token (a word or piece of a word) in a text is coloured by where its 64 most similar tokens sit: in another text, in the same text more than 64 tokens away, or in the same text within 64 tokens. Similarity compares two tokens' current states and, with memory, their recent pasts; a memory knob sets how far back the comparison looks. Click any token to inspect its neighbours as text and check the colour yourself.

Model and layer

Two models ({{fact:x.viewer.arch}} each) read the exact same tokens. Layer chooses which block's output state is compared: after block 4, 8, 12, or 16 (layer 16 is taken before the final normalisation).

Memory: comparing two pasts end to end

Two tokens are compared by lining up their histories at the right end and comparing them step by step going back, with older steps counting less. Memory sets how far back the comparison looks: none (current token only), about 2, about 8, about 64 tokens, or the whole past equally weighted. Each token's state first has one average removed and is scaled to length 1, so comparison is by angle (cosine). Because each step pairs matching lags rather than averaging each past, two neighbouring tokens are not similar just because their pasts overlap. Two effects follow: a match carries over to the next token along the text, so neighbours appear in runs; and at the whole past, unequal history lengths push tokens toward similar positions even in random text.

Settings: what is subtracted, what is barred

One average removed subtracts the average hidden state across all texts (one average per model and layer). Each text's own average removed (offered with nearby tokens barred) subtracts that document's whole average—including later tokens; it can also remove topic-related differences. Nearby tokens barred forbids neighbours within 64 tokens in the same text, so orange vanishes by rule rather than as a finding.

Neighbours and where they sit

Each token's neighbours are the 64 most similar among all {{fact:x.viewer.pool_tokens}} analysed tokens (or only its own tokens for a spliced text). Colour blends the three locations by their shares; the card lists the closest ({{fact:x.viewer.shown}} shown; 64 counted).

Ties

If several tokens tie at the 64th distance, the list fills remaining slots in text-and-position order and marks them ≈. Model states almost never tie; ties matter mainly for the text-only controls in the experiments. Comparisons between neighbour lists treat tied places fairly rather than using that order.

How to check a claim

Click any non-grey token. The card shows its counts and its {{fact:x.viewer.shown}} nearest neighbours as text, with their similarity and the model's next-token predictions. Each row shows a token (the selected one, or a neighbour) and its 23 predecessors, lined up at the end. Use go → (same text) or open → (other text) to inspect a neighbour.
